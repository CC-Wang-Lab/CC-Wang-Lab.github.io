About

The laboratory, the people who lead it, and how companies work with us.

What we can do for a project

Research says what we study. This says what we can measure, model, build and validate for you.

Experimental thermal engineering

Purpose-built rigs for measuring heat transfer and pressure drop under controlled conditions.

  • Pool and flow boiling rigs, including enhanced and porous surfaces
  • High-speed visualization of bubble dynamics and onset of nucleate boiling
  • Fin-and-tube and compact heat exchanger test loops
  • Refrigerant and dielectric working-fluid handling
  • Calibrated instrumentation with documented uncertainty

Electronics & data-center testing

Thermal characterization from a single package up to a populated rack.

  • Cold-plate and heat-sink performance characterization
  • Single-phase and two-phase immersion cooling evaluation
  • Rack and server airflow measurement and management
  • Heat-pipe and vapor-chamber module testing
  • Package-level thermal resistance measurement

Modeling & simulation

Component and system models, validated against our own measurements.

  • CFD of airflow, conjugate heat transfer and two-phase flow
  • System-level thermal and energy models
  • Correlation development from measured data
  • Design-space exploration and optimization
  • Uncertainty quantification and sensitivity analysis

AI-assisted engineering

Machine learning applied where a physical model alone is too slow or too uncertain.

  • Physics-informed neural network surrogate models
  • Smart thermal control, including reinforcement learning
  • Fouling and performance-degradation forecasting
  • Data-driven correlation and model calibration

Industrial R&D at scale

Experimental, computational and AI-assisted thermal engineering across electronics cooling, data centers, HVAC&R and thermal systems.

Data centers & AI hardware

Cold plates, immersion tanks, rack airflow, cooling energy, waste-heat recovery

Semiconductor & packaging

High heat flux at package level, thermal resistance, hot spots

HVAC&R & heat pumps

Heat exchanger performance, frost and defrost, dehumidification, high-temperature heat pumps

Electric vehicles

Battery and power-electronics thermal management

Many industrial collaborations are confidential. Organizations are named publicly only where disclosure has been approved.

What a collaboration looks like

Most projects follow the same four steps. The first one is free.

  1. You describe the problem. What you are cooling, what the heat load is, what is currently stopping you. One email or one meeting is enough.

  2. We say honestly whether we can help. If the answer is no, we say so and, where we can, point you somewhere better.

  3. We agree a scope. Measurement only, modeling only, or both. Timeline, deliverables and confidentiality are fixed in writing before work starts.

  4. We deliver, and we defend the numbers. Every measurement comes with its uncertainty. Every model is validated against data.

Why this laboratory

  • About 60 researchers. Enough people to run a serious company project without stopping the rest of the lab.

  • Experiment and simulation under one roof. We do not hand you a model nobody has validated.

  • Twelve patents. The group has taken ideas past publication and into protectable technology.

  • Twenty-one years of industrial R&D. Prof. Wang led research at the Industrial Technology Research Institute from 1989 to 2010 before returning to academia. This laboratory was built by somebody who has worked to industrial deadlines.

Working with a university, practically

  • Confidentiality. A non-disclosure agreement is normal and expected. Most of our industrial work is covered by one.

  • Publication. We will always ask before publishing anything from a joint project, and we accept a delay or a refusal.

  • Intellectual property. Terms are agreed in the contract before work begins, not afterwards.

  • Cost. University overheads are lower than a commercial test house, and the work is done by researchers who publish in the field.

Have a thermal problem?

Tell us what you are trying to cool, and what is currently stopping you. We will tell you honestly whether we can help.
