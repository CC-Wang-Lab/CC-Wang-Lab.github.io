About

An overview of the laboratory's research capabilities, leadership and approach to industrial collaboration.

Research capabilities

The laboratory measures, models, builds and validates thermal systems for research and industrial applications.

Experimental thermal engineering

Purpose-built rigs for measuring heat transfer and pressure drop under controlled conditions.

  • Pool and flow boiling rigs, including enhanced and porous surfaces
  • High-speed visualization of bubble dynamics and onset of nucleate boiling
  • Fin-and-tube and compact heat exchanger test loops
  • Refrigerant and dielectric working-fluid handling
  • Calibrated instrumentation with documented uncertainty

Electronics & data-center testing

Thermal characterization from a single package up to a populated rack.

  • Cold-plate and heat-sink performance characterization
  • Single-phase and two-phase immersion cooling evaluation
  • Rack and server airflow measurement and management
  • Heat-pipe and vapor-chamber module testing
  • Package-level thermal resistance measurement

Modeling & simulation

Component and system models, validated against our own measurements.

  • CFD of airflow, conjugate heat transfer and two-phase flow
  • System-level thermal and energy models
  • Correlation development from measured data
  • Design-space exploration and optimization
  • Uncertainty quantification and sensitivity analysis

AI-assisted engineering

Machine learning applied where a physical model alone is too slow or too uncertain.

  • Physics-informed neural network surrogate models
  • Smart thermal control, including reinforcement learning
  • Fouling and performance-degradation forecasting
  • Data-driven correlation and model calibration

Industrial collaboration

The laboratory applies experimental, computational and AI-assisted methods to electronics cooling, data centers, HVAC&R and thermal systems.

Data centers & AI hardware

Cold plates, immersion tanks, rack airflow, cooling energy, waste-heat recovery

Semiconductor & packaging

High heat flux at package level, thermal resistance, hot spots

HVAC&R & heat pumps

Heat exchanger performance, frost and defrost, dehumidification, high-temperature heat pumps

Electric vehicles

Battery and power-electronics thermal management

Many industrial collaborations are confidential. Organizations are named publicly only where disclosure has been approved.

Collaboration process

Most collaborations follow four stages. The initial consultation is provided without charge.

  1. Problem definition. The organization describes the system, heat load, temperature limit and current technical constraint. An email or an initial meeting is sufficient.

  2. Capability assessment. The laboratory determines whether its experimental and computational capabilities are appropriate. If they are not, the laboratory states that clearly and, where possible, identifies a more suitable resource.

  3. Scope agreement. Experimental work, modeling or a combined program is defined together with the schedule, deliverables, confidentiality terms and intellectual-property provisions.

  4. Delivery and validation. Measurements include documented uncertainty, and models are validated against experimental data.

Laboratory strengths

  • Approximately 60 researchers. The group has the capacity to conduct substantial industrial projects alongside its academic research.

  • Integrated experimentation and simulation. Experimental measurements and computational models are developed and validated within the same laboratory.

  • Twelve patents. The group's record includes twelve patented technologies.

  • Twenty-one years of industrial R&D experience. Professor Wang worked at the Industrial Technology Research Institute from 1989 to 2010 before joining National Chiao Tung University as a professor.

Practical terms for university collaboration

  • Confidentiality. Non-disclosure agreements are standard, and most industrial work is conducted under one.

  • Publication. Material arising from a joint project is submitted to the partner for approval before publication; delayed publication or non-publication can be accommodated.

  • Intellectual property. Ownership and licensing terms are defined in the written agreement before work begins.

  • Cost. Project costs and university overheads are defined in the written agreement before work begins.
