Boiling & Two-Phase Heat Transfer

Pool boiling on porous surfaces

High-speed visualization of nucleate boiling on a 65% porosity surface in R1233zd, measuring how the surface changes the onset of boiling and the heat transfer coefficient.

Eiusmod Tempor
PhD Student

The question

A boiling surface removes far more heat than a dry one, but only once boiling actually starts. On a smooth surface that start is late and unpredictable. A porous coating gives vapor somewhere to nucleate, so boiling begins earlier and at a lower wall superheat.

How much earlier, and at what cost in pressure drop, is what this rig measures.

The rig

A sealed chamber holding R1233zd, with a heated test surface at the bottom and a high-speed camera looking horizontally across it. The chamber runs at controlled saturation pressure; the surface under test is a copper substrate with a sintered porous layer of 65% porosity.

Measured on every run:

  • wall superheat, from thermocouples embedded below the surface

  • applied heat flux, from the cartridge heater power

  • saturation pressure and temperature in the chamber

  • bubble departure diameter and frequency, from the high-speed footage

What the footage shows

The video on the home page of this site is from this rig, at 2.5 bar. Watch the first second: the surface is quiet, then nucleation sites switch on across the whole face almost together. That near-simultaneous onset is the porous layer working. On a plain surface the sites appear one at a time over several degrees of superheat.

Status

Data collection is in progress. Results are not published yet.
